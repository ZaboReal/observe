Verify. Record. Permit.

Your product's border control for agents: check every agent's passport, keep the entry log, and let your customers issue visas for exactly what their agents may do.

  1. 01

    See every agent

    One script tag tells when an agent is driving, names the product, and marks the moment a person hands the session over. It reads how the page is driven, never what is typed or shown.

  2. 02

    Trust in tiers

    Agents that sign their requests are verified. Known products are recognised from how they behave. Everything else is unknown automation, and it gets the least.

  3. 03

    A record of who really acted

    Every agent action is logged with the agent, its visa and the person it acted for. Ready for SOC 2 audits, regulated customers and the EU AI Act.

  4. 04

    Visas your customers control

    People approve their agent once, in plain words. Admins choose which agents their team may use. Visas expire on their own and revoke in one click.

  5. 05

    A decision on every sensitive action

    Exports, invites, payments and settings get one call: allow, ask the person, slow down, bill or refuse. Asking uses their passkey, which no agent can approve.

Common questions

Still have one? Write to us. A founder will read it.

What counts as an AI agent?

Anything that drives your product for a person: browser extensions like Claude in Chrome, AI browsers like Comet, agents built into Chrome and Edge, cloud agents like ChatGPT agent, and automation frameworks. Our catalogue names 70 of them; anything else that is clearly automated is still caught as unknown automation.

How is this different from bot protection?

Bot protection asks whether this is a bot; your login asks whether this is the right person. An in-browser agent passes both: it runs on your customer's own device, with their cookies and their account. We answer the question that's left: which agent is acting, for whom, and is it allowed?

What do you collect?

How the page is driven: pointer paths, click and key timing, how text and scrolling arrive, and markers agents leave on the page. Never what people type or see, and no screenshots.

Will it get in my users' way?

Nothing is blocked unless you write a rule that blocks it. Most teams start by seeing which agents are there, then turn on visas for two or three sensitive actions.

How do you recognise agents that don't identify themselves?

Many leave exact markers on the page. For the rest, a decision model reads how the session is driven and compares it with what we know about each agent. Agents that sign their requests are verified cryptographically and get the highest tier.

Is this only about stopping bad agents?

No. Most agent use is your customers getting work done. Visas let you give good agents a smooth path, see which features they rely on, and price their use fairly, while keeping the risky actions behind your customers' approval.
