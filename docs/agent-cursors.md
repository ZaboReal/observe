# How AI browser agents move the cursor

Research notes, 8 October 2026. Question: when an AI agent drives a browser, does it move a cursor, and what does the page's JavaScript see?

Short answer: almost none of them move a cursor the way a hand does. Nearly every agent in use today does one of these, in falling order of how common it is:

1. **One jump ("teleport").** A single `mousemove` lands on the target, usually its exact centre, then the button goes down and up a few milliseconds later. This is the default for Stagehand/Browserbase, Claude in Chrome, ChatGPT and Codex in Chrome, Comet, Gemini in Chrome, browser-use, Skyvern, Manus, Playwright, Puppeteer and the computer-use reference harnesses. It does not matter whether the events come from the DevTools protocol (CDP `Input.dispatchMouseEvent`), from Chrome's own agent code, or from the operating system (`xdotool`, PyAutoGUI): the path is still one jump.
2. **No pointer events at all.** Text set through value setters, `element.click()`, scrolling with `scrollTo`, accessibility actions. Most agents fall back to this for some actions (form fills, occluded elements, scrolling).
3. **A straight line.** Playwright or Puppeteer `mouse.move(x, y, { steps })`, Firefox WebDriver moves, PyAutoGUI with a `duration`. Equal steps, constant speed. Only when a script asks for it.
4. **Human-like curves.** ghost-cursor, Camoufox `humanize`, Steel `humanize_interactions`, Browserless "humanlike". All opt-in add-ons. None of the big consumer agents (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Comet, Muse) and nothing in the Browserbase stack does this.

The FP-Agent study (May 2026) measured seven agents (Atlas, browser-use, Claude, Comet, Skyvern, ChatGPT agent, Manus) on a honey site and found the same thing for all of them: they "produce mouse events only at click time", one move followed straight away by the press and release.

Driving the real OS cursor does not make an agent look human. Anthropic's reference computer-use harness warps the X11 pointer with `xdotool mousemove` and clicks with a 12 microsecond gap between press and release.

### What a teleport looks like, measured

Our lab run on 8 Oct 2026 used headless Chrome 154 on macOS and playwright-core 1.63. The button is 200x60 px, its centre is (500, 330), and each driver clicked it once.

| Driver | Moves before press | Press (down to up) | `pressure` on down | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Playwright `locator.click()` | 1 | 0 ms | 0.5 | exact centre |
| Raw CDP, pipelined (Stagehand v3/v4) | 1 | 0.2 ms | 0 | exact centre |
| Raw CDP with sleeps (browser-use) | 1, then 53 ms of hover | 80 ms | 0 | exact centre |
| Playwright `mouse.move(steps: 20)` | 21, each 30 px and 16.7 ms apart | 1.8 ms | 0.5 | a straight line at constant speed |
| `element.click()` | 0 | none | none | one untrusted `click`: `clientX` 0, `detail` 0, `pointerId` -1, `pointerType` "" |

Other things we saw:

- All CDP events were `isTrusted: true`.
- The first `pointermove` on a page has `movementX/Y = 0`. A later jump puts the whole distance in one event; one jump measured `movementX` 450 and `movementY` 290.
- `screenX` was `clientX + 22`. Before Chrome 142, CDP mouse events had `screenX === clientX` (the [fix landed in 142.0.7417](https://chromium-review.googlesource.com/c/chromium/src/+/6917162)), so that check now only catches old Chromium builds.
- One CDP `mouseWheel` gives one `wheel` with `deltaY` 300. `synthesizeScrollGesture` gave four `wheel` events with fractional deltas (43.1, 89.2, 137.0, 30.6), which looks like trackpad scrolling.

## What a real person's cursor looks like

- **Many moves, paced by the screen.** Chrome (since version 60) holds `mousemove` and `pointermove` until just before the next animation frame. Safari also merges each frame's moves into one event (see WebKit 235116 below). A moving hand therefore produces roughly one event per frame in Chrome and Safari: about 60 a second on a 60 Hz display, or 120 on ProMotion. Other browsers may pace differently. Extra samples are in `getCoalescedEvents()` (Chrome 58+, Firefox 59+, Safari 18.2+; secure contexts only). A person also produces moves between clicks, while reading, and on the way to the target. Agents produce moves only at click time. Over a whole session, mouse events per second is the strongest single cue (see the July 2026 preprint below).
- **Curved paths and changing speed.** A fast first movement, then slowing, with small corrections near the target. Paths often overshoot and come back, and speed follows a bell-shaped curve rather than a constant rate. Hand-made curves (Bézier, WindMouse) still differ from this in velocity and jerk, which is why cside could separate them.
- **They land off-centre and pause first.** People click wherever the cursor stops inside the target, rarely on its exact centre, and there is usually a visible settle before the press.
- **Press duration.** A mouse press usually lasts around 100-120 ms, give or take about 30 ms; automated presses are under 10 ms and nearly identical every time (preprint arXiv:2607.26935). Trackpad tap-to-click is the big exception: a WebKit engineer describes the gap between mousedown and mouseup on a tap as "essentially zero".
- **Pressure.** A real mouse with no pressure sensor reports `pressure` 0.5 while a button is down; that is what the Pointer Events spec requires. CDP sends 0 unless the driver passes `force`.
- **Safari's `movementX/Y` is not reliable.** Before Safari 15.5, `pointermove` always reported 0 ([WebKit 220194](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=220194)). iOS `pointermove` lacked the fields until a 2023 fix ([WebKit 255207](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=255207)). Today Safari merges each frame's moves into one event but keeps only the last delta in `movementX/Y`, so the sum comes out at about one twelfth of the real distance. That was reported on Safari 26.3.1 on 5 Oct 2026, and the bug is still open ([WebKit 235116](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=235116)). Do not compare `movementX` with `clientX` deltas in Safari.

### Humans who look synthetic (false-positive traps)

1. **Touch screens (phones, tablets, touch laptops).** There is no hover and no movement before the press. After the finger lifts, the compatibility events `mouseover`, `mousemove`, `mousedown`, `mouseup` and `click` fire together at one point, so mousedown to mouseup is about 0 ms. On iOS, quick taps were historically held back about 300 ms. That is a perfect "teleport" if you only listen to mouse events. Check `pointerType: "touch"` and the touch events first.
2. **Trackpad tap-to-click.** The press is close to 0 ms with a normal path before it. A short press alone is not agent evidence.
3. **Keyboard and screen readers.** Enter or Space on a button, NVDA or JAWS browse mode, and VoiceOver VO-Space all fire `click` with no pointer events before it. Chrome and Firefox report `clientX/Y` 0, `detail` 0, `pointerId` -1 and `pointerType` "". **Safari puts the coordinates at the element's exact centre** ([WebKit 281430](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=281430), open, and WebKit says it will keep this for now). That looks exactly like a centred teleport click. Look for the `keydown` that came before.
4. **Label clicks in Chrome.** When a real mouse clicks a `<label>`, the `click` forwarded to its input has `pointerType` "" and `pointerId` -1 ([w3c/pointerevents #554](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-pointer-events/2025OctDec/0002.html)).
5. **Safari's `movementX`**, as above.
6. **No movement because none was needed.** A dialog or button appears under a cursor that is already still, or the user clicks "+" five times in place. That is a press with no move before it.
7. **Remote desktop, VDI, cloud PCs, screen sharing with remote control.** Moves arrive less often and in bigger, laggier jumps, so the path looks less fluent than a local hand ([Schneier on remote-access mouse dynamics](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2015/03/identifying_whe.html)).
8. **A human taking over an agent's cloud browser.** Browserbase live view ("take control"), Muse takeover and ChatGPT agent takeover feed the person's input through the same remote channel the agent uses. The motion is human-shaped, but it can carry the channel's artifacts, for example CDP pressure 0, and the same session holds both agent and human stretches. Score short windows, not whole sessions.
9. **Accessibility pointer tools.** [Mouse Keys](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mh27469/mac) moves the cursor from the keypad in eight fixed directions, in straight runs, and clicks from a key. Eye trackers and head pointers with dwell clicking, Switch Control, and Voice Control's "click X" or mouse grid all produce jumps, long dwells or odd presses. Do not block on cursor shape alone.
10. **Pens and tablets.** `pointerType: "pen"`. The cursor jumps when the pen comes into range, and absolute-mode tablets jump across the screen.
11. **Smooth scrolling is not proof of a human.** CDP `synthesizeScrollGesture` (used by ChatGPT in Chrome and by browser-use) makes several `wheel` events with fractional deltas that look like a trackpad (see the lab above).

## What this means for detection

- **Strong agent evidence, rare in people** (still combine signals):
  - `pointerdown` with `pointerType: "mouse"` and `pressure: 0`. This is CDP without `force`: Stagehand v3/v4, browser-use, Puppeteer, ChatGPT and Codex in Chrome.
  - Pointer events with no screen position (`screenX` 0 while `clientX` is above 0), as Gemini in Chrome's hover moves have.
  - `screenX === clientX` on Chromium below 142.
  - Known DOM cursor overlays: `#__v3_cursor_overlay__`, `#__browse_cursor_overlay__`, `#claude-phantom-cursor`, `#__pw-cursor`, and the rest in [agent-registry.md](agent-registry.md).
- **Teleport clicks are the common case. Score the combination, not one part:** a mouse (not touch, pen or keyboard), at most two moves, a jump of more than about 80 px, landing within a pixel of the element's centre, pressed 15 ms or less after arriving, with a press of 6 ms or less. Each part alone happens to people; all of them together almost never do.
- **Session-level rates beat single clicks.** Agents emit mouse events only when they click, so mouse events per second, and the share of clicks reached by a teleport, separate agents even when the paths are humanised ([arXiv:2607.26935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26935): those two features gave 100% agent recall across its evasion levels; [FP-Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01247)).
- **Fixed timing is a tell even inside the human range.** browser-use always waits about 50 ms and 80 ms, Gemini in Chrome 5 ms and 5 ms. Look at how much the timing varies across clicks, not just its average.
- **Straight lines at constant speed** (equal steps, equal spacing) mean Playwright or Puppeteer `steps`, or Firefox WebDriver. Mouse Keys users make straight runs too, so treat this as medium weight.
- **Do not count as agent evidence on their own:** a short press (trackpad, touch), a click with no pointer events (keyboard, assistive tech), centred coordinates in Safari, odd `movementX` in Safari, coarse jumpy motion (remote desktop), fractional wheel deltas. Check `pointerType`, a preceding `keydown`, and the browser before scoring.
- **Humanised paths are rare but rising.** Bézier and WindMouse are still caught by trained models. Camoufox's replayed real recordings are the hard case; for those, rely on the session-level rates above, timing against page changes, and fingerprint consistency.
- **Use identity where it exists.** Browserbase, ChatGPT agent, Google-Agent, Manus, Anchor and Kernel sign requests with Web Bot Auth, which is cheaper and decisive. Muse and, as far as anyone has published, Instinct do not sign, look like stock Chrome, and are still expected to teleport, so pointer behaviour is the main signal for them.
